% ======================= Abstract =======================
\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Informal, cash-based services such as plumbing, electrical work, carpentry and painting
form the backbone of household maintenance in many African cities, yet they remain largely
unorganized, unverified and difficult to access. \textbf{FundiLink} is a mobile-first platform
that bridges the gap between everyday customers and qualified local artisans (\emph{Fundis})
by applying the trusted concepts of a gig economy--matching, transparent pricing, escrow
payments and verified reviews--to a market that is traditionally served through word of mouth.
This report describes the problem being solved, the proposed solution, the core subsystems
that make it work, and the design decisions behind the end-to-end service lifecycle from
service request to payment release.
\end{abstract}

% ======================= 1. Introduction =======================
\section{Introduction}

Across East Africa, and Uganda in particular, the vast majority of trade in skilled manual
labour takes place outside formal marketplaces. A household in Kampala needing a leaking
roof fixed, a faulty electrical socket repaired, or a wardrobe built must rely on personal
referrals, roadside labour markets, or informal notice boards. Each of these channels shares
a set of fundamental weaknesses:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{No trust or verification} --- the customer cannot easily tell whether an
          artisan is qualified, insured, or reliable before work begins.
    \item \textbf{No price transparency} --- quotes are opaque, often negotiated verbally,
          and there is little accountability when costs escalate during a job.
    \item \textbf{No protected payment} --- payment is made in cash before or during work,
          leaving customers exposed to poor workmanship and artisans exposed to non-payment.
    \item \textbf{No feedback loop} --- past performance is invisible, so good artisans
          struggle to build reputations and customers cannot learn from others' experiences.
    \item \textbf{Poor discoverability} --- location and availability are not indexed, making
          it hard to find a skilled, nearby artisan quickly in an emergency.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{FundiLink} was conceived to address exactly these problems. It treats a skilled
artisan the way modern platforms treat drivers, tutors and freelancers: verified profiles,
transparent pricing, escrow-protected transactions, live progress tracking, and reviewable
outcomes. By moving the informal service market onto a structured digital platform, FundiLink
aims to build trust where none previously existed and to make skilled labour more accessible,
safer, and fairly paid.

% ======================= 2. Problem Statement =======================
\section{Problem Statement}

The central problem is the \textbf{absence of a trusted, structured marketplace for skilled
manual services} in the local market. This manifests as several interconnected difficulties:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Trust asymmetry.} Customers must commit money and home access to strangers
          based on little verifiable information. Artisans must likewise trust customers to
          pay for completed work. There is no neutral mechanism to reduce this mutual risk.
    \item \textbf{Discovery and matching.} Finding an available, nearby, appropriately
          skilled artisan at short notice is inefficient and error-prone.
    \item \textbf{Pricing disputes.} Without agreed-upon quotes or a recorded transaction,
          price disagreements are common and unresolved.
    \item \textbf{Payment security.} Cash or informal transfer offers no protection for either
          party if the job is not performed to the agreed standard.
    \item \textbf{Reputation opacity.} A well-performing artisan has no portable reputation,
          while a poorly performing one faces no consequence, distorting the market.
    \item \textbf{Emergency responsiveness.} When urgent repairs are needed (e.g. a burst
          pipe), customers often cannot locate an available professional on demand.
\end{enumerate}

These problems create a fragmented and risky service market. The objective of this project is
to design and build a system that resolves them through a \emph{structured, trust-building
service lifecycle} delivered on a mobile application.

% ======================= 3. Proposed Solution =======================
\section{Proposed Solution}

FundiLink answers the problem with a \textbf{two-sided mobile platform}, supported by an
administration web dashboard, that orchestrates the complete journey of a service engagement.
The core idea is to move the informal handshake into a governed, step-by-step workflow in
which risk is managed at each stage.

\subsection{The Trusted Service Lifecycle}

The platform formalizes each engagement as a \textbf{structured booking lifecycle} with clear,
accountable stages:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Request.} The customer describes a problem, optionally in plain language
          assisted by an artificial-intelligence classifier that suggests the correct service
          category, attaches photos and a location, and submits a request.
    \item \textbf{Matching.} The system ranks candidate Fundis by a weighted combination of
          their rating and their distance from the customer, notifies nearby eligible Fundis
          in real time, and guides them through an accept/decline flow with a response timer.
    \item \textbf{Price agreement.} Before work begins, both parties negotiate and formally
          agree on a price, recording the agreement so that later disputes are minimized.
    \item \textbf{Escrow-protected payment.} The agreed amount is held securely in an escrow
          account before work commences, protecting the customer from paying for incomplete
          or poor work.
    \item \textbf{Execution \& live tracking.} The customer can follow the Fundi's live
          location and the evolving status of the job (on the way, arrived, in progress).
    \item \textbf{Completion \& review.} Upon confirmed completion, funds are released to the
          Fundi (net of a platform commission), and the customer leaves a rating and review
          that builds the Fundi's public reputation.
    \item \textbf{Dispute resolution.} In cases of disagreement, an administrator can review
          the engagement and either release or refund the escrowed funds.
\end{enumerate}

By treating payment, reputation and progress as explicitly managed -- rather than assumed --
elements of the service, the platform converts an informal transaction into a governed,
trustworthy exchange.

The solution is delivered as a modular, three-component system, each responsible for a
distinct layer of the platform.

\subsection{Component View}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Mobile client (two modes).} A single application provides both a
          \emph{Customer} mode (request, book, pay, track, review) and a \emph{Fundi} mode
          (accept jobs, update status, negotiate price, withdraw earnings), allowing users to
          operate on either side of the market.
    \item \textbf{Admin dashboard.} A web client gives platform operators tools to verify
          Fundis, review disputes, release or refund escrow, configure fees, and observe
          analytics.
    \item \textbf{Backend API.} The core orchestrator, organized into focused modules for
          authentication, jobs and bookings, fundi matching, payments and wallets and escrow,
          chat and notifications, reviews and ratings, AI classification, plus maps and
          location services.
    \item \textbf{Supporting infrastructure.} MongoDB for persistence, Socket.IO for
          real-time updates, external SMS and geocoding providers, and an AI service for
          natural-language understanding and message generation.
\end{itemize}

% ======================= 5. Core Subsystems =======================
\section{Core Subsystems}

The behaviour of the platform rests on several carefully designed subsystems. Each is
summarized below in terms of the problem it solves within the overall service.

\subsection{Authentication and Identity}
Onboarding is designed to be efficient and inclusive of users who may not have an email
address. A phone-first flow delivers a one-time password (OTP) via SMS, while an optional
Google sign-in streamlines access and provides verified identity data. A role-picker lets a
single user operate as a customer, a Fundi, or both, and Fundis additionally pass through a
verification step that builds customer confidence.

\subsection{Service Matching and Recommendation}
The matching engine answers ``which Fundi should be offered this job?'' by combining two
signals that matter most to customers: \emph{quality} (aggregate rating) and
\emph{proximity} (distance). A weighted score
\[
    \text{Score} \;=\; 0.6 \cdot \text{Rating} \;+\; 0.4 \cdot \text{Proximity}
\]
ranks candidate Fundis, with proximity normalized so that closer artisans score higher. The
shortlist of nearby, category-matched Fundis is notified in real time.

\subsection{Booking and Job Management}
Bookings model the full state machine of an engagement from \textsc{Pending}, through
\textsc{Accepted}, \textsc{On the Way}, \textsc{Arrived}, \textsc{In Progress}, to
\textsc{Completed}, with explicit \textsc{Cancelled} and \textsc{Disputed} terminal states.
A response timer manages Fundi availability, and price negotiation records a formal agreed
amount before work proceeds. This structured state is what makes tracking, payment release
and dispute resolution well-defined.

\subsection{Wallets, Payments and Escrow}
Financial trust is the most delicate part of the platform and is handled by a dedicated
subsystem:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Wallets.} Each user maintains a wallet capturing an available balance and
          a held (escrow) balance, enabling deposits, transfers and withdrawals via mobile
          money.
    \item \textbf{Escrow.} On price agreement, the agreed amount is held rather than paid
          out, protecting the customer.
    \item \textbf{Release.} On confirmed completion the escrow releases the Fundi's payout
          and the platform retains a commission (a client service fee). A remaining held
          balance is tracked so funds are never lost.
    \item \textbf{Refund.} On cancellation or dispute, escrowed funds are returned to the
          customer.
    \item \textbf{Ledger.} Every movement is recorded as a typed transaction with a
          reference, giving an auditable trail that powers wallet history and administrative
          oversight.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Real-Time Chat and Notifications}
Communication is kept in-context. Booking-centric conversations let the customer and Fundi
clarify requirements without leaving the workflow, while Socket.IO drives real-time booking
alerts, status changes and location updates. This immediacy is crucial for live tracking and
for informing Fundis of new opportunities the moment they appear.

\subsection{Reviews and Ratings}
Reputation is the currency of trust on the platform. Reviews capture a star rating,
a written comment, optional service details and photos. A deliberate design rule is that a
customer retains \emph{one consolidated review per Fundi}: re-engaging a previous Fundi
updates the existing review rather than creating duplicates, keeping the Fundi's rating
honest and clutter-free.

\subsection{AI Assistant and Classification}
To lower the barrier to entry, a natural-language classifier interprets plain descriptions
(e.g. ``my sink is leaking'') and suggests the correct service category with a confidence
score, steering customers to the right Fundis. AI assistance is also used to generate
natural SMS copy for service events, keeping users informed without manual authoring.

\subsection{Administration and Governance}
A web dashboard gives operators the tools to govern the market: verifying Fundis, resolving
disputes, releasing or refunding escrow, configuring platform fees and settings, and viewing
aggregate analytics. This governance layer is what gives the two-sided marketplace its
accountability and keeps financial operations auditable.

% ======================= 6. Methodology =======================
\section{Methodology}

The project follows an iterative, prototype-driven methodology suited to a fast-moving,
multi-actor system:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Problem discovery.} Interviews and domain analysis in the target market
          identified trust, discovery, pricing and payment as the dominant friction points.
    \item \textbf{Service design.} The engagement was decomposed into its natural stages
          (request $\rightarrow$ match $\rightarrow$ agree $\rightarrow$ pay $\rightarrow$
          execute $\rightarrow$ complete $\rightarrow$ review), which became the booking
          state machine.
    \item \textbf{Prototype development.} A functional mobile prototype was built end-to-end
          across all three components, with each subsystem implemented, integrated and
          exercised against the live backend.
    \item \textbf{Feedback and iteration.} Real usage surfaced usability and data-integrity
          issues (for example duplicate reviews when a customer re-hired the same Fundi),
          which were corrected through targeted design changes rather than one-off fixes.
    \item \textbf{Validation.} Core scenarios -- booking, payment, live tracking, review and
          admin governance -- were validated end-to-end against the deployed environment.
\end{enumerate}

% ======================= 8. Conclusion and Future Work =======================
\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

FundiLink demonstrates that the trust-building mechanics of the gig economy can be applied
directly to the informal skilled-labour market. By structuring each engagement as a governed
lifecycle--service request, intelligent matching, recorded price agreement, escrow-protected
payment, live execution, and reviewable completion--the platform resolves the core problems
of trust asymmetry, opaque pricing, insecure payment, poor discoverability and invisible
reputation that have long hindered this market.

Future work can extend the current foundation in several directions:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Real payment-gateway integration} to the platform's wallet and escrow,
          replacing simulated mobile-money deposits with genuine, verifiable transactions.
    \item \textbf{Enhanced recommendation signals.} Incorporating response time, job
          completion rate and in-demand category weighting into the matching score.
    \item \textbf{Advanced analytics.} Segmenting platform usage and earnings to surface
          market-level insights to administrators.
    \item \textbf{Localisation.} Deepening multi-language support beyond English and Luganda,
          and expanding coverage across additional regions.
    \item \textbf{Trust-safety hardening.} Automated document verification and fraud
          detection to strengthen the governance layer as the user base grows.
\end{itemize}
