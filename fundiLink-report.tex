% ======================= 1. Executive Summary =======================
\section{Executive Summary}

In many African cities, skilled manual services -- plumbing, electrical work, carpentry and
painting -- are essential to daily life, yet they remain largely informal, unverified and
difficult to access. A homeowner with a leaking roof, a faulty socket or a broken wardrobe
must rely on word of mouth, personal referrals, or roadside labour markets. None of these
channels offer assurance of quality, transparency of price, or security of payment.

\textbf{FundiLink} is a mobile platform that organises this informal market into a trusted
marketplace. It connects everyday customers to vetted local artisans ("Fundis"), gives them
transparent, agreed pricing, and protects both sides of every transaction through an escrow
payment system. In short, it applies the trust built into modern gig-economy platforms to a
sector that has so far been left untouched by them.

This document explains the problem in detail, the opportunity it presents, how FundiLink
solves it, and the investment required to bring this solution to market.

% ======================= 2. The Problem =======================
\section{The Problem}

The skilled-service trade in the region is the backbone of household and small-business
maintenance. Yet it suffers from deep, structural weaknesses that harm customers, artisans
and the wider economy.

\subsection{An Unorganised and Unverified Market}

Today, finding a trustworthy artisan is a gamble. There is no directory, no registry of
qualified professionals, and no way for a customer to verify a worker's skill, training or
reliability before letting them into their home. As a result, both good and poor artisans
look identical at the point of hiring.

\subsection{Opaque and Unfair Pricing}

Service prices are rarely fixed or agreed in advance. Quotes are given verbally, often vary
wildly between providers for the same work, and escalate once work has begun. Without a
written or recorded agreement, customers have little recourse when the final bill does not
match the original promise.

\subsection{Insecure Payment on Both Sides}

Payment in this market is usually in cash, before or during the work. This arrangement
leaves the customer exposed to poor, incomplete or abandoned workmanship, and leaves the
artisan equally exposed to customers who refuse to pay for completed work. There is no
neutral third party to protect either side.

\subsection{No Reputation, No Accountability}

Past performance is effectively invisible. A skilled artisan who does excellent work cannot
build a portable reputation or attract new customers on merit, while a worker who performs
badly faces no consequence. This removes the incentive for quality and keeps the market
crowded with uncertainty.

\subsection{Slow and Frustrating Discovery}

When a repair is urgent -- a burst pipe, a failed electrical circuit -- customers struggle
to locate an available, nearby professional quickly. There is no reliable way to know who is
open, who is close, and who is qualified.

Taken together, these problems mean that a basic, universally needed service is delivered
inefficiently, riskily and without fairness for anyone involved.

% ======================= 3. The Opportunity =======================
\section{The Opportunity}

The weaknesses described above are not merely inconveniences; they describe a large,
underserved and growing market that is ready for structure.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, label={--}]
    \item \textbf{Rapid urbanisation} is steadily increasing the number of homes owned,
          rented and maintained, and with it the demand for reliable repair and improvement
          services.
    \item \textbf{Smartphone adoption} in the region continues to grow quickly, bringing a
          large share of households within reach of a mobile-first service.
    \item \textbf{Existing payment habits} already include mobile money, meaning users are
          comfortable transacting digitally and are ready for secure, cashless payment.
    \item \textbf{Proven model elsewhere.} Booking, matching, escrow and review platforms
          have transformed transport, accommodation and freelance work elsewhere. The same,
          proven playbook has not yet been applied seriously to skilled manual services in
          this market.
\end{itemize}

FundiLink sits at the intersection of these four trends. It is not asking users to adopt a
new behaviour; it is organising behaviour they already practise -- hiring artisans and paying
via mobile money -- into a safer, fairer and more efficient form.

% ======================= 4. Our Solution =======================
\section{Our Solution}

FundiLink is a two-sided mobile platform connecting customers with vetted local artisans,
supported by an administrative dashboard. At its heart is a simple idea: turn an informal,
risky handshake into a structured journey that protects both parties at every step.

\subsection{How It Works}

Each engagement on FundiLink follows a clear, accountable sequence:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Request.} The customer describes the problem, optionally in plain language,
          attaches photos and a location, and submits a request.
    \item \textbf{Matching.} The system ranks nearby artisans by a combination of their
          rating and their distance, and notifies the best-fit candidates in real time.
    \item \textbf{Agreed pricing.} Before work begins, both parties negotiate and formally
          agree on a price, removing ambiguity about the cost.
    \item \textbf{Secure payment.} The agreed amount is held in escrow, protecting the
          customer from paying for work that is not done, and protecting the artisan from
          non-payment.
    \item \textbf{Live progress.} The customer can follow the artisan's location and the
          stage of the job (on the way, arrived, in progress).
    \item \textbf{Completion and release.} Once the job is confirmed complete, funds are
          released to the artisan, and the customer leaves a rating and review.
    \item \textbf{Resolution.} If anything goes wrong, an administrator can review the case
          and release or refund the escrowed funds fairly.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Trust Loop}

The lasting value of FundiLink is the feedback loop it creates. Every completed job builds a
public record of each artisan's reliability and quality. Over time, good artisans are
rewarded with more work and higher standing, poor artisans are naturally filtered out, and
customers gain a trustworthy, searchable pool of professionals. This turns an uncertain
market into one governed by reputation -- the single strongest force for quality.

\subsection{Designed for the Local Market}

The platform is deliberately built around local realities: phone-first identity and sign-in
for users without email, mobile-money payments, a local-language interface (including
Luganda), and a featureset that fits the way services are actually bought and sold here.

% ======================= 5. The Asking =======================
\section{What the Funding Is For}

FundiLink is currently at a functional prototype stage. The core platform -- the mobile
application, the backend, the payment and escrow logic, and the administrative dashboard --
has been built and demonstrated end to end. Investment will take it from a working prototype
to a dependable, scaled service. Funds will be directed to four areas.

\subsection{Bringing Payments to Production}

Today escrow and wallet features are implemented with simulated transactions. Funding will
connect the platform to genuine mobile-money payment gateways, enabling real deposits,
payments and payouts. This is the single most important step toward a trusted, working
service, and the primary focus of initial investment.

\subsection{Launching in a First City}

Funding will support a focused launch in a single target city: recruiting and verifying a
critical mass of artisans, building initial customer demand, and running the promotional and
operational activities needed to reach the point where the marketplace becomes self-sustaining.

\subsection{Building Engineering Capacity}

The current team is small. Investment will grow the engineering and operations team, speed
up development, and fund the continuous maintenance, security and support a live financial
service requires.

\subsection{Extending and Hardening the Product}

Further work will deepen local-language support, add stronger trust-and-safety protections,
and improve the analytical tools that help administrators run the platform well as the user
base grows.

% ======================= 6. Why Now / Summary =======================
\section{Conclusion}

There is a large, urgent and currently unserved need for a trusted way to find, hire, pay and
review skilled artisans. The habits that would make such a service work -- widespread mobile
money, growing smartphone use, and comfort with booking platforms -- are already in place.
What is missing is a platform that brings them together for skilled manual work.

FundiLink is that platform. It has been designed, built and proven as a prototype, and it is
ready to be taken to production with the right support. We would welcome the opportunity to
discuss the project in more detail, to share a live demonstration, and to explore a
partnership with you.
